Drosophila melanogaster · FlyWire v783

I gave a fruit fly's brain twelve LeetCode problems

The whole wiring diagram of an adult fly brain, 138,639 neurons, run as a frozen network. Problems go in through its taste, touch and smell neurons one symbol at a time. Answers are read off the neurons that carry commands down to its body. The only thing trained is that final reading.

It was Accepted on three of twelve, the three whose answers are really counts. The more interesting results are why my first readout only got one, and what happened when I scrambled its wiring.

How a fly reads a problem

Symbols in through the senses, answers out through the body

Each symbol a problem can contain, such as ( or 7, gets its own fixed set of 40 sensory neurons. Showing the fly "([)]" means stimulating four of those sets in order. Between symbols there is a short silence, so "11" and "1" look different.

The connections come from FlyWire's proofread v783 release, the same data behind the Eon Systems emulation. Every connection with at least five synapses is kept. That leaves 2.7 million connections, each signed excitatory or inhibitory by its predicted neurotransmitter. After the last symbol, the network settles briefly and a ridge regression reads the answer from the 1,303 descending neurons. None of the brain's weights change.

Inside the brain

Sixty descending neurons reading two strings

Rows are 60 of the brain's descending neurons, and columns are simulation steps, four per symbol plus six to settle. Left is a valid string and right is an invalid one. They share their first two symbols, and the activity splits within a few steps of the third one arriving.

Limits

What this is not

  • It is a rate model. Each neuron is a smooth number between −1 and 1. Shiu et al. and Eon Systems use spiking neurons, which are closer to biology and much slower to simulate.
  • At the gain used here the whole brain lights up at once. That regime is useful for computing but it is not how a real fly brain behaves.
  • The problems are scaled down to what fits in a few symbols. Two Sum uses four numbers from 0 to 9.
  • The fly produces answers, not code, so nothing here can be submitted to LeetCode itself.
  • The readout is trained separately for each problem. There is no single fly that knows all twelve.
